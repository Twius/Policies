## 3. What Sifdroid does {#what-it-does}

Sifdroid is a record-keeping tool. It documents money you have already moved through your own bank, broker, or exchange, and it is built so that it cannot do anything else.

Sifdroid does not:

- execute, place, or settle any trade, transfer, or payment of your money, and it has no ability to do so. The only purchase it can start is Sifdroid Pro, which Google Play handles (see [section 7](#purchases));
- connect to your bank, broker, exchange, or card, and it cannot read your balances or transactions from them;
- hold money. It is not a bank, a broker, a custodian, or a payment service.

Every number the app shows is worked out from what you entered. Your account balances, net worth, budget totals, and portfolio values are arithmetic on your own entries and on prices and exchange rates from third parties. If an entry is missing, duplicated, or mistyped, the totals will be wrong, and the app has no way of knowing.

When your accounts, assets, or holdings use different currencies, the app converts them into your base currency using published reference rates. Income and expenses use the rate for the day they happened, or the nearest day the app has a rate for until that one downloads. What you paid for an investment uses the rate for the day you bought it. Balances, assets, holdings, and net worth use today's rate. These rates can differ from the one your bank, broker, or card provider actually used. On the budget, accounts, and assets screens, an amount the app has no rate for is left out of the total until one is available. The budget and assets screens tell you when this happens.
